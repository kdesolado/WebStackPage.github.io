Supplement choices, with context

A practical starting point for comparing common supplements. Food comes first, evidence matters, and individual needs should be discussed with a qualified health professional.

Common options worth discussing

These are ingredient categories, not blanket prescriptions or endorsements of a specific brand. The purchase slots remain inactive until verified Amazon Associate links are supplied.

Bone and muscle health

Vitamin D

Vitamin D helps the body absorb calcium. A blood test can help determine whether you are getting enough from food, sunlight, and supplements.

  • More relevant when deficiency or low sun exposure is a concern.
  • Avoid high-dose products unless a clinician recommends them.
Read the NIH fact sheet
Nerves and muscles

Magnesium

Magnesium supports many body processes. Food sources include legumes, nuts, seeds, whole grains, and leafy greens.

  • Some supplemental forms are absorbed more readily than others.
  • Supplements can cause diarrhea and interact with some medicines.
Read the NIH fact sheet
Dietary fats

Omega-3

Fatty fish and seafood are preferred food sources of EPA and DHA. Fish oil, krill oil, and algal oil are common supplement forms.

  • Supplements are not routinely recommended for everyone.
  • Ask a clinician first if you use anticoagulants or have heart disease.
Read the NIH fact sheet
Possible nutrient gaps

Basic multivitamin

A basic multivitamin may help when food alone does not provide enough nutrients, but it cannot replace a varied diet.

  • Prefer age- and life-stage-appropriate products near recommended amounts.
  • Avoid doubling up nutrients across multiple supplements.
Read the NIH fact sheet
